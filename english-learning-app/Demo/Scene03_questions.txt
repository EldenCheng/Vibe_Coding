Level1:
Q1. Look at the picture. Where are the students? What time is it? How do you know?

Q2. Look at the food counter. What food can you see? Name at least four kinds of food.

Q3. What is on Li Ping's tray?

Q4. Look at the poster on the wall. What does it say? What can you see in the picture on the poster?

Level2:

Q1. Look at Li Ping's tray and Wang Ming's tray. Whose food is healthier? Why do you think so? (Use "because...")

Q2. Look at the menu board. How much is the fish? How much are the hamburgers? How much are they together?

Q3. Susan is only having a bowl of salad and a glass of juice. Do you think that is a good lunch for a student? Why or why not?

Q4. Look at the other students in the canteen. What are they doing? Describe the busy lunchtime in the canteen.

Q5. What do the students in this picture usually eat for lunch? Use "often", "sometimes" and "never" in your answer.

Level3:

Q1. The poster says "EAT WELL, LIVE WELL, BE WELL". In your opinion, what is a balanced diet for a high school student?

Q2. Do you think fast food, like hamburgers and French fries, should be sold in a school canteen? Why or why not?

Q3. Imagine you work in this canteen. Wang Ming wants to eat healthily. What would you say to him? (You can start with: "You'd better..." or "Why not try...")

Q4. If you could design your own lunch tray in this canteen, what food would you choose? Why do you think it is a balanced meal?

Q5. Think about your own eating habits. Did you use to eat different food when you were in primary school? How have your eating habits changed?

Q6. Some students skip meals to lose weight, like Susan may be doing. What would you say to a classmate of yours who wants to look slim but often feels tired in class?
