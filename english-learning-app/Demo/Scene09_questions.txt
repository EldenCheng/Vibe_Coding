Level1:
Q1. Look at the picture. What clubs can you see? Name all three clubs.

Q2. Look at the boy on the left. What is he doing? What instrument is he playing?

Q3. Look at the girl in the middle. What is she doing? What can you see on her easel?

Q4. Look at the table in front. What does the small sign say? What is the student doing at the table?

Level2:

Q1. Read the three posters. Which club meets on Friday? Which club meets on Monday?

Q2. The tall boy on the right is spinning a basketball on one finger. Which club do you think he wants to join? Why?

Q3. Look at the pictures on the three posters. What can you see in each picture?

Q4. Count the students in the picture. How many students can you see? What is each of them doing?

Q5. If your teacher asks you to join one club, which one will you choose? Why?

Level3:

Q1. The girl can paint and the boy on the left can play the guitar. What about you? What can you do?

Q2. Do you think it is a good idea for a student to join two clubs? Why or why not?

Q3. The school wants to start a new club (for example, a chess club or an English club). What club would you like to start? Why?

Q4. The art club meets on Monday and the basketball club meets on Wednesday. A student wants to learn to paint AND play basketball. Can he join both clubs? How do you know?

Level4:

Q1. Imagine you are at the club fair and you want to join the Music Club. Ask the guitar boy two questions about the club. (You can start with: "Excuse me, when does the club meet?")

Q2. You are the guitar boy. A new student comes to your booth. Talk about what you can do and invite him to join your club.

Q3. Imagine you want to start a robot club at your school. Talk to your teacher and give two reasons why the school needs this club.
